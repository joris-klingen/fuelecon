Nine million cars

How much fuel efficiency actually improved in the Dutch passenger car fleet, and how many of each car is left on the road. Every figure is drawn from the full vehicle registry: 9.5 million cars joined to 16.9 million fuel and emissions records.

190 → 71 g CO2/km Mean tailpipe CO2, build year 2000 against 2024 — a 62 percent fall. Most of the drop after 2019 is electrification rather than better engines.

What the fleet looks like

Nine and a half million passenger cars built between 2000 and 2024 are registered in the Netherlands today.

The largest surviving vintage is 2019, with 581 thousand cars still on the road. Just over a fifth of the fleet was built in 2000-2009. Volkswagen is the largest make at 11.7 percent, and the Volkswagen Polo the most numerous single nameplate at 283 thousand cars.

Across the whole 2000-2024 fleet the powertrain split is petrol 71.3 percent, self-charging hybrid 8.1, diesel 7.7, battery-electric 6.5, plug-in hybrid 5.6, and LPG 0.7. Read those as the composition of what survives, not of what was ever sold.

Diesel is the row that looks wrong and is not. Its average rises because diesel retreated to heavy vehicles: the mean kerb mass of a new diesel went from 1,669 kg in 2019 to 2,538 kg in 2024. Over the same years the mean petrol car got lighter, from 1,203 kg to 1,128 kg, as the larger cars electrified. Neither series is a clean reading of engine development on its own.

Four ways to get this wrong

Each of these produces a confident, plausible, incorrect answer. All four are handled in the transformation step and documented where they are handled.

Stock, not sales

The registry is a snapshot of what is registered today. Every count is a survival count, so the pre-2008 tail says nothing about how many cars were sold in those years.

NEDC is not WLTP

Type approval switched cycles between September 2017 and September 2018, and WLTP returns figures 15 to 25 percent higher for a physically identical car. A single series across the switch shows an efficiency regression that never happened.

Electric cars have no CO2 record

RDW leaves the CO2 field null for battery-electric cars rather than writing a zero. Aggregating over non-null CO2 therefore drops precisely the cars that are displacing combustion, and the fleet average stops meaning anything after about 2020.

Hybrids share a fuel list

A plug-in hybrid and a self-charging hybrid carry identical fuel rows. Only klasse_hybride_elektrisch_voertuig separates them. Classifying on the fuel list alone moves 775 thousand self-charging hybrids into the plug-in bucket and roughly triples it.

Reproduce it

Nothing here is committed as data. Both commands run from a clean checkout. 

uv sync
uv run fuelecon all           # download, build the warehouse, export CSV
Rscript R/run_analysis.R      # figures and headline numbers

The download takes about twelve minutes over the RDW API and is resumable. DuckDB does the row crunching once; R reads the resulting aggregate tables and draws the figures in CPB house style via ggcpb.

Source: RDW open data, datasets m9d7-ebf2 and 8ys7-d773, retrieved 13 August 2026. Zero tailpipe emissions are zero at the tailpipe only; generation emissions sit outside the registry's scope.
